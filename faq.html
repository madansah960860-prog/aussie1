Frequently asked questions

The questions that arrive most often, grouped by subject. If yours is not here, send us a message and we will answer and usually add it to this page.

Using the recipes

Yes, throughout. One Australian cup is 250 ml and one Australian tablespoon is 20 ml, which is four teaspoons rather than the three used in the United States, the United Kingdom and New Zealand. Teaspoons are 5 ml everywhere. The full comparison is on our kitchen basics and conversions page, and the reasons it matters are covered in this article.

The toggle sits in the sticky bar under the header on every recipe page. Switching it converts every ingredient quantity on the page at once, using the Australian 250 ml cup. Ingredients that only make sense by weight, such as a whole pumpkin or a piece of chuck steak, stay in grams and kilograms because a cup measurement of them would be meaningless.

Yes. Every recipe has a servings stepper above the ingredient list, and changing it recalculates all the quantities. Two things it cannot do: it will not resize your tin, and it will not adjust cooking times. As a rough guide, doubling a tray bake usually needs two trays rather than a longer cook, and halving a cake usually needs a smaller tin and about a quarter less time.

Most domestic ovens are 10 to 20°C away from the temperature on the dial, and the error is not consistent across the range. Every recipe here gives a conventional temperature with the fan-forced equivalent beside it, fan-forced normally being 20°C lower. An oven thermometer costs about $15 AUD and tells you what your oven actually does. There is more detail in our guide to reading your oven.

Usually, and most recipes list the swaps we have actually tested under a swaps and variations heading. Be more careful in baking, where flour, raising agents, sugar and fat are doing structural work. Swapping self-raising flour for plain flour, or icing mixture for pure icing sugar, will change the result noticeably.

Almost everything on this site is available at Coles, Woolworths or Aldi. Asian pantry items are cheaper and usually better at an Asian grocer, and Middle Eastern staples such as tahini, freekeh and good olives are cheaper at a Mediterranean or Middle Eastern grocer. Where a recipe needs something genuinely specialist, we say so and give a substitute.

Dietary needs, nutrition and allergens

They are estimates, calculated from the listed ingredients, and they are labelled as estimates on every recipe. They exclude optional ingredients and anything served alongside. Real values vary with brands, portion size and substitutions. Nutrition information on this site is general information only and is not dietary advice. See the nutrition and allergen disclaimer.

Treat them as a starting point, not as a clearance. We list the common allergens present in the recipe as written, but we cannot know what brands you buy, what is in your pantry, or how your kitchen is set up. Always read the packaging on the products in your own kitchen, and if you or someone you cook for has a diagnosed allergy or intolerance, speak to a qualified health professional.

Yes. The recipe index has filters for vegetarian, vegan, dairy-free option and gluten-free option. A recipe is only marked with a dietary tag when it is either already suitable as written or has a specific swap we have tested and written into the recipe.

No. We are cooks, not dietitians or medical professionals. You will not find claims about weight loss, detoxing, immunity, healing or any therapeutic effect anywhere on this site, because we are not qualified to make them and they would not be true. For advice about your own diet or health, see a doctor or an Accredited Practising Dietitian.

Printing, saving and the site itself

Yes. The Print button in the bar under the header uses a print stylesheet that strips out the navigation, the images you do not need, the advertising slots and the footer, leaving the ingredients, method, times and notes. Nothing downloads to your computer and no window opens. It simply sends the page to your normal print dialogue.

It stores the recipe reference in your own browser, on your own device, using local storage. Nothing is sent to us, and nothing is stored until you click it. Clearing your browser data will clear the list, and the list does not follow you to another device. Saved collections that sync across devices are part of the optional membership.

No, deliberately. Ticks last for as long as the page is open and are cleared when you reload. They are there so you can keep your place while shopping or cooking, and nothing about them is stored or sent anywhere.

Please link to it rather than copying it. You are welcome to share the link anywhere, print a copy for your own kitchen, and cook the recipes for anyone including in a commercial kitchen. What you may not do is republish the text or the photographs on another site or in a publication without written permission. The details are in our terms of use.

Tell us and we will fix it. The site is built to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA, with semantic landmarks, a skip link, visible focus states and full keyboard operation, but real users find things that testing misses. Email access@sunlitpantry.com.au or read our accessibility statement.

Because it will say so at the very top of the page, above the recipe, in a labelled box. Not in the footer, not in small print at the end.
