BBQ & Outdoor

Written for the barbecue most Australian households actually own: a four burner hooded gas barbecue with a grill on one side and a flat plate on the other. Charcoal instructions are included where they change the outcome.

Two zones, and a hood that closes

The single biggest improvement most people can make is to stop cooking everything over full heat across the whole barbecue. Turn the two burners on one side to high and leave the other side off. Now you have a hot zone for colour and a cool zone for finishing, and you can move food between them instead of standing there hoping.

The second is to use the hood. With the lid down, a barbecue behaves like an oven and the temperature gauge means something. Chicken pieces, whole fish and pizza all need that surrounding heat. With the lid up, only the surface touching the metal is cooking.

Clean while it is hot. Turn the burners to high for five minutes after you take the food off, then scrape the plate with a metal scraper. Cold barbecue plates are miserable to clean, which is why so many of them never get cleaned.

Finally, on fire safety: keep the barbecue well away from the house and anything overhanging, never leave it unattended while lit, and check your local council and fire authority rules during a total fire ban. In most Australian states, gas barbecues have specific conditions on ban days.

Barbecued Corn with Lime and Smoked Paprika Butter

Husks off, straight onto the bars, and the butter goes on afterwards rather than before so it does not flare up. The flavoured butter can be rolled and frozen weeks ahead.

Method

  1. Make the butter

    Mash the softened butter with the smoked paprika, lime zest, garlic, salt flakes and coriander. Roll it into a log in baking paper and refrigerate for 15 minutes, or freeze for later.

  2. Heat the barbecue

    Heat the grill bars to medium-high. Corn burns quickly on a screaming hot barbecue and stays raw inside.

  3. Oil the corn, not the bars

    Rub the cobs lightly with neutral oil. Oiling the corn rather than the grill means less smoke and fewer flare-ups.

  4. Turn every two minutes

    Grill for 8 to 10 minutes, turning a quarter turn every 2 minutes, until each side has dark blistered patches and the kernels are bright yellow.

  5. Butter off the heat

    Move the corn to a platter and rub slices of the cold butter over the hot cobs so it melts into the kernels. Serve with lime wedges.

Cook's tips

  • Buy corn in its husk. Pre-husked corn on a foam tray dries out. Peel back a strip and check the kernels are plump and pale gold, not shrunken. Australian corn is at its best from about November to March.
  • Do not salt the corn before grilling. Salt draws moisture to the surface, which steams the kernels instead of charring them. It goes in the butter instead.
  • Make double butter. It keeps for a month in the freezer, sliced straight from frozen onto steak, fish or baked potatoes.

Storage and make ahead

The butter keeps 2 weeks refrigerated and 3 months frozen. Cooked corn keeps 2 days, and leftover kernels cut off the cob are excellent in fritters or salads. Do not freeze cooked corn on the cob, the texture goes watery.

Allergens. Contains dairy. Gluten free as written. Use a plant-based spread for a dairy free version. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Beetroot and Beef Burgers with Tarragon Mayo

The Australian burger, which means tinned beetroot, and no apology for it. The only real technique is handling the mince as little as possible, because compressed mince makes a dense, bouncy patty.

Method

  1. Form the patties lightly

    Divide the mince into four and shape each portion into a patty about 2 cm thick and slightly wider than the bun. Handle it as little as possible. Press a shallow dimple into the centre of each so they do not dome.

  2. Season the outside only

    Season the patties on both surfaces with the salt and pepper right before they go on. Salt mixed through mince ahead of time changes the texture towards sausage.

  3. Mix the mayo

    Stir the mayonnaise, mustard, tarragon and lemon juice together and refrigerate.

  4. Drain the beetroot properly

    Lay the beetroot slices on paper towel for 10 minutes. Wet beetroot is what turns a burger bun into pink mush.

  5. Cook on the flat plate

    Heat the flat plate to high with the oil. Cook the patties for 4 minutes, flip once, add the cheese, and cook another 3 to 4 minutes. Do not press them with the spatula, that squeezes out the juices.

  6. Toast the buns and build

    Toast the cut buns on the plate for 1 minute. Spread both halves with mayo, then build: lettuce, patty with cheese, tomato, beetroot, top. The lettuce underneath protects the base from the juices.

Cook's tips

  • Ask the butcher for chuck mince. Supermarket premium mince is often too lean, around 5 per cent fat, and lean mince makes dry burgers. You want closer to 20 per cent.
  • The dimple is not optional. Patties contract as they cook and puff up in the middle. A shallow thumbprint in the raw patty keeps them flat, which means the fillings stay on.
  • Rest for 3 minutes. Straight off the plate, the juices run out into the bun. Three minutes on a warm plate makes a noticeable difference.
  • Flat plate, not grill bars. Bars let the fat drip into the flame and flare, which coats the meat in soot. The flat plate keeps the fat where it does some good.

Storage and make ahead

Raw patties can be shaped a day ahead, layered between baking paper and refrigerated, or frozen for 2 months. The mayo keeps 4 days in the fridge. Cooked patties keep 2 days and are good chopped through a salad. Assembled burgers do not keep.

Allergens. Contains gluten (bun), egg (mayonnaise), dairy (cheese) and mustard. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Barbecued Chicken Pizza with Red Onion

A covered barbecue runs hotter than a domestic oven, which is exactly what a pizza base wants. Use bought bases or dough from the supermarket bakery and this is a thirty minute job.

For the bases

For the topping

0 of 9 ticked

Method

  1. Heat the barbecue with the hood down

    Turn all burners to high, close the hood and let it reach about 250°C, which takes 10 to 15 minutes. Then turn the middle burners off to make an indirect zone.

  2. Cook the chicken first

    If using raw chicken, grill the thighs for 5 minutes a side over direct heat, rest, then slice. Leftover roast chicken skips this step entirely.

  3. Prepare the bases

    Dust a flat tray or pizza peel with semolina and lay the base on it. Brush the rim with olive oil. Semolina acts like ball bearings and lets the pizza slide off.

  4. Top lightly

    Spread a thin layer of passata, leaving a 2 cm border. Add mozzarella, chicken, red onion and oregano. Overloading is the main reason home pizzas go soggy in the middle.

  5. Cook over the indirect zone

    Slide the pizza onto the barbecue over the switched-off burners, close the hood, and cook for 8 to 10 minutes until the base is crisp and the cheese is blistered. Rotate once halfway.

  6. Finish and rest

    Scatter with rocket, rest for 2 minutes, then cut. Cutting immediately drags the cheese off the base.

Cook's tips

  • Use a pizza stone or steel if you own one. Heat it with the barbecue for a full 20 minutes. It gives the base the sharp bottom heat that makes the difference between good and very good.
  • Slice the onion paper thin. Thick onion will still be raw and harsh in ten minutes. A mandolin or a very sharp knife, and thinner than feels necessary.
  • Do not put the rocket on before cooking. It burns to ash in under a minute at 250°C. Leaves go on at the end, always.

Storage and make ahead

Cooked chicken can be grilled a day ahead. Assembled pizza does not keep raw, the base goes soggy within 20 minutes. Leftover cooked pizza keeps 2 days and reheats best in a dry frying pan with a lid, not a microwave.

Allergens. Contains gluten and dairy. Read the packaging in your own kitchen, particularly bought bases.

Charred Vegetable Platter with Whipped Feta

Zucchini, capsicum and eggplant cooked hard and dressed while still hot, so they drink up the vinegar instead of sitting in it. This is the dish that makes vegetarians at a barbecue feel like guests rather than an afterthought.

To dress

Method

  1. Salt the eggplant

    Lay the eggplant rounds on a rack, salt both sides with half the salt flakes and leave for 15 minutes. Pat dry. This seasons it and firms the flesh so it absorbs less oil.

  2. Oil the vegetables

    Brush all the vegetables with olive oil and season with the remaining salt. Oil the food, not the barbecue.

  3. Whip the feta

    Blitz the feta, yoghurt and lemon juice in a food processor for 90 seconds until smooth and airy. If you have no processor, mash it with a fork and beat hard with a whisk.

  4. Grill in batches

    Cook on a hot grill plate: capsicum skin side down for 8 minutes, eggplant 4 minutes a side, zucchini 3 minutes a side, onion 5 minutes a side. Move each one off when it is charred and just tender.

  5. Dress while hot

    Pile the vegetables straight into a bowl and toss with the red wine vinegar and crushed garlic while still hot. They will absorb the dressing as they cool.

  6. Assemble

    Spread the whipped feta across a large platter, pile the dressed vegetables on top and scatter with torn mint. Serve at room temperature.

Cook's tips

  • Cook each vegetable separately. They finish at different times and a mixed pile means half of it is raw and half is collapsed. It takes longer but it is the whole difference.
  • Steam the capsicum after grilling. Put the charred pieces in a bowl covered with a plate for 10 minutes and the blackened skin lifts off with your fingers.
  • Room temperature, not cold. Straight from the fridge, the olive oil goes cloudy and the flavours flatten. Take it out 30 minutes before serving.

Storage and make ahead

The whole platter can be made a day ahead and improves overnight. Store the vegetables and the feta separately in the fridge for up to 3 days and assemble on the day. Add the mint at the last minute. Do not freeze.

Allergens. Contains dairy. Gluten free as written. For a vegan version, replace the whipped feta with a thick hummus. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.

Barbecued Whole Snapper with Lemon and Oregano

Cooking a whole fish on the barbecue is much easier than it looks, and much cheaper per kilo than fillets. The two things that matter are a very clean, very hot plate and the confidence to leave it alone.

4 serves

4

Australian 250 ml cups.

For the fish

For the dressing

0 of 10 ticked

Method

  1. Score and season

    Pat the fish dry. Cut three diagonal slashes through the thickest part of each side, down to the bone. Rub oil and salt flakes over the skin and into the slashes.

  2. Stuff the cavity

    Fill the cavity with lemon slices, oregano sprigs and parsley. Do not overfill it, the fish needs to sit flat on the plate.

  3. Clean and heat the plate

    Scrape the flat plate clean, heat it to high, then oil it with a folded piece of paper towel held in tongs. A dirty or cool plate is why fish sticks.

  4. Cook the first side

    Lay the fish down and close the hood. Cook for 12 minutes without touching it. If it does not release when you slide a spatula under it, give it another minute.

  5. Turn once

    Use two spatulas, one at the head end and one at the tail, and turn it in a single confident movement. Cook the second side for 10 to 12 minutes with the hood down.

  6. Check and rest

    The flesh at the thickest slash should be opaque and pull away from the bone. Rest for 5 minutes, then whisk the dressing and pour it over. Serve with lemon wedges.

Cook's tips

  • The slashes are not decoration. They let heat into the thickest part so the shoulders finish at the same time as the belly, and they give you somewhere to check for doneness without wrecking the presentation.
  • Ask the fishmonger to scale and gut it. They will do it in ninety seconds. Scaling a fish in a home kitchen puts scales in places you will find for weeks.
  • Buy what looks good, not what the recipe says. Snapper, bream, whiting and barramundi all work. Clear eyes, firm flesh and a clean smell matter far more than the species.
  • A fish basket helps. If turning a whole fish makes you nervous, a hinged wire fish basket costs about $25 AUD and removes the problem entirely.

Storage and make ahead

The dressing can be made 3 days ahead. Cooked fish keeps 1 day in the fridge and is very good flaked through a salad the next day. Do not freeze cooked fish. Buy the fish on the day you plan to cook it.

Allergens. Contains fish. Gluten free and dairy free as written. Read the packaging in your own kitchen.
