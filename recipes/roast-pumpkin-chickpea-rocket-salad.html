Roast Pumpkin, Chickpea and Rocket Salad

By Marlee Ashcroft. Published 05/08/2026. Tested twice.

A warm salad substantial enough to be dinner. The chickpeas roast on the same tray as the pumpkin and turn crisp, the rocket wilts slightly under the hot vegetables, and the tahini dressing keeps in the fridge for a week.

  • Vegetarian
  • Contains sesame, dairy

Use Kent pumpkin and leave the skin on

Kent, sold as jap pumpkin in most Australian greengrocers, roasts to a dense, sweet flesh and its skin softens enough to eat. Butternut works too but goes watery if the oven is not hot enough. Queensland blue is superb roasted but the skin stays tough, so peel that one.

Skin on is not laziness. It holds the wedge together on the tray, it browns beautifully, and it adds fibre. Scrub the pumpkin under the tap, dry it, and cut it into wedges about 3 cm at the thickest point.

The chickpeas need to be properly dry before they go in the oven. Drain the tin, tip them onto a clean tea towel, and roll them around for thirty seconds. Wet chickpeas steam and stay soft, which is fine but is not what this salad wants.

Method

  1. Heat the oven

    Heat the oven to 220°C, or 200°C fan-forced. Line the largest tray you own with baking paper. A crowded tray steams, so use two trays rather than piling everything onto one.

  2. Season the tray

    Toss the pumpkin wedges and the dried chickpeas with the olive oil, cumin, smoked paprika and salt flakes. Spread them out so nothing overlaps, with the pumpkin cut side down.

  3. Roast

    Roast for 30 minutes, turning the pumpkin once at the 20 minute mark. You are looking for dark caramelised edges on the pumpkin and chickpeas that rattle slightly when you shake the tray.

  4. Make the dressing

    Whisk the tahini, lemon juice, garlic and honey in a bowl. It will seize and look ruined. Keep whisking and add the cold water a tablespoon at a time until it loosens into a pourable cream. This always happens and it always comes back.

  5. Soften the onion

    Toss the sliced red onion with a squeeze of extra lemon juice and a pinch of salt. Leave it for 10 minutes while the tray finishes. It turns pink and loses its raw bite.

  6. Assemble while warm

    Spread the rocket over a large platter. Tip the hot pumpkin and chickpeas straight on top so the leaves wilt a little underneath. Scatter with feta, pepitas and the pink onion.

  7. Dress and serve

    Spoon the tahini dressing over in ribbons rather than tossing it through, so you get pockets of leaf, dressing and pumpkin instead of a uniform beige salad. Serve immediately with the extra dressing on the table.

Cook's tips

  • The dressing will seize. Do not panic. Tahini stiffens the moment acid hits it, then relaxes as you add water. Cold water works better than warm, and adding it slowly gives a glossier result.
  • Dry the chickpeas like you mean it. Thirty seconds in a tea towel is the difference between crisp chickpeas and soft ones. Any skins that come loose can go in the bin, they burn first.
  • Dress at the table if it needs to travel. Take the tahini in a jar and the salad undressed. This is what makes it a good dish to bring to someone else's house.
  • Save the pumpkin seeds. Rinse the seeds from the middle, dry them, toss with oil and salt, and roast them alongside for 12 minutes. Free pepitas.

Storage

Undressed components keep separately in the fridge for 3 days. Store the roast pumpkin and chickpeas in one container and the dressing in a jar. Assembled salad does not keep well because the rocket collapses, so build only what you plan to eat.

The tahini dressing lasts a week in the fridge. It will thicken as it sits, so loosen it with a spoonful of water before using.

Make ahead

Roast the pumpkin and chickpeas in the morning and leave them at room temperature. Warm them for 8 minutes at 180°C, or 160°C fan-forced, before assembling. The dressing can be made 5 days ahead and the pepitas toasted a week ahead.

Make it vegan

Leave out the feta and use maple syrup instead of honey. To replace what the feta was doing, add 2 tablespoons of capers, rinsed and fried in a little oil until they burst open, and an extra pinch of salt flakes. Nothing else changes.

Allergens

Contains sesame (tahini) and dairy (feta). The vegan version contains sesame only. Chickpeas are a legume, which some people react to. Always read the packaging on the products in your own kitchen and speak to a health professional about any dietary or medical need.
