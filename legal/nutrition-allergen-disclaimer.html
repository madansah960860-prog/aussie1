Nutrition & Allergen Disclaimer

Nutrition figures on this site are estimates. Allergen tags are a starting point, not a clearance. Neither is a substitute for reading the packaging in your own kitchen or speaking to a qualified health professional.

Last updated 15/08/2026. Version 1.0.

Please note. This legal copy is a starting template and must be reviewed by an Australian-qualified lawyer before launch. It is not legal, medical or dietary advice.

1. Nutrition information is an estimate

Where a recipe on this site shows a nutrition panel, those figures are estimates only. They are calculated from the ingredients as listed and are provided for general information.

Estimates will differ from what you actually eat, sometimes substantially, because:

  • brands vary. Two tins of the same product can differ in sodium, sugar and fat;
  • produce varies. A large onion and a small onion are both one onion;
  • portions vary. A recipe that serves four serves four different amounts depending on who is at the table;
  • substitutions change everything, and we encourage substitutions;
  • optional ingredients, garnishes, oil left in the pan and anything served alongside are generally not counted;
  • cooking itself changes composition, particularly for fat that renders out or liquid that evaporates.

Do not rely on these figures for medical, clinical or therapeutic purposes, for managing a diagnosed condition such as diabetes or coeliac disease, or for any purpose where precision matters. Speak to a doctor or an Accredited Practising Dietitian instead.

During site development, some nutrition values on this site are placeholders and are marked as such in the page source. Every figure must be replaced with a verified analysis before launch.

2. Allergen information

Recipes on this site list the common allergens we are aware of in the recipe as written. In Australia, the allergens requiring declaration on packaged food include peanuts, tree nuts, milk, eggs, sesame, fish, crustacea, molluscs, soy, lupin, wheat and other cereals containing gluten, added sulphites at 10 mg per kilogram or more, and, for some products, certain others.

Our allergen tags are a starting point for your own checking, not a clearance. We cannot know:

  • which brands you buy, or what those brands have changed since we tested;
  • what precautionary statements appear on your packaging, such as may contain traces;
  • what else is in your kitchen, on your boards, in your oil, or on your equipment;
  • what substitutions you have made.

Always read the label on every product you use, every time. Manufacturers reformulate without warning, and the packet in your hand is the only reliable source.

3. If you are cooking for someone with an allergy

Food allergy can be life threatening. If you are cooking for someone with a diagnosed allergy or intolerance:

  • ask them what they need, rather than assuming from a tag on a website;
  • read every label yourself, including on staples you have used before;
  • be aware of cross contamination from shared boards, utensils, toasters, fryers and oil;
  • follow any management plan they have been given by their doctor or allergy specialist;
  • if in doubt, do not serve it.

For general information about food allergy in Australia, speak to your GP, a clinical immunologist or allergy specialist, or an Accredited Practising Dietitian. Organisations including Allergy & Anaphylaxis Australia and the Australasian Society of Clinical Immunology and Allergy publish resources for households and for schools.

4. We make no health or medical claims

We are cooks, not dietitians, nutritionists or medical practitioners. Nothing on this site is dietary advice, nutritional advice or medical advice, and nothing here should be used to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition.

You will not find claims on this site that a food or recipe is healthy in a therapeutic sense, that it will help you lose weight, that it detoxifies anything, that it boosts immunity, or that it treats or prevents illness. We do not make those claims because we are not qualified to and because, for the most part, they are not true.

Where we describe a dish as light, fresh, rich or filling, we are describing how it tastes and eats, not making a nutritional statement.

5. Dietary labels: what they mean here

How we use dietary tags
TagWhat it means on this site
VegetarianNo meat, poultry, fish or seafood in the recipe as written. May contain dairy, eggs and honey. Note that some cheeses, including traditional parmesan, are made with animal rennet.
VeganNo animal products in the recipe as written, including no honey.
Vegan optionA specific, tested swap is written into the recipe that makes it vegan.
Dairy-free optionA specific, tested swap is written into the recipe that removes dairy.
Gluten-free optionA specific, tested swap is written into the recipe. You must still check every product, especially stock, soy sauce, oats and baking powder.

A recipe carrying a tag has not been tested in a certified allergen-free facility or laboratory. The tag describes the ingredients we used, nothing more.

6. Food safety

Cooking times and temperatures in our recipes are a guide based on testing in one home kitchen. Your equipment will differ. Use the visual and temperature cues given in each recipe, and make sure food is cooked through before serving.

General food safety guidance appears on our kitchen basics page. For authoritative advice, see Food Standards Australia New Zealand and your state or territory health department.

Some groups, including people who are pregnant, very young children, older adults and people who are immunocompromised, are advised to avoid certain foods such as raw or undercooked eggs, raw seafood and some soft cheeses. If that applies to you, follow current advice from your doctor or health department rather than a recipe website.

7. Alcohol

A small number of recipes include alcohol as an ingredient, such as wine in a braise. Not all of it cooks off. Where a recipe contains alcohol we say so, and we give a substitution where one works. Do not serve those dishes to anyone avoiding alcohol without making the substitution.
