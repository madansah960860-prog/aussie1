Cookie Policy

What we store on your device, why, and how to change it. No non-essential cookie is set before you make a choice.

Last updated 15/08/2026. Version 1.0.

Please note. This legal copy is a starting template and must be reviewed by an Australian-qualified lawyer before launch. It is not legal advice.

1. What a cookie is

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store on your device. It lets a site remember something between page loads, such as whether you have already dismissed a banner. Related technologies do similar jobs: local storage keeps information in your browser without sending it back to the server, and pixels are tiny image requests used to record that a page was viewed.

This policy covers all of them, and forms part of our Privacy Policy.

2. Our approach

We set as few cookies as we can get away with, and none of the optional ones before you tell us it is alright.

  • On your first visit you see a banner with three choices: Accept all, Reject non-essential and Manage preferences.
  • Nothing beyond essential storage happens until you choose. Rejecting is one click, in the same place, in the same size button as accepting.
  • You can change your mind at any time using the Cookie settings link in the footer of every page.
  • Rejecting non-essential cookies does not restrict any part of the site. Every recipe, tool and page works either way.

3. Essential storage, always on

These are needed for the site to function and do not require consent. They store no advertising or tracking identifiers.

4. Analytics cookies, with consent

If you accept analytics, we use them to understand in aggregate which recipes people actually use, how long they stay and where they came from. This tells us what to cook next. It is not used to identify you personally.

5. Advertising cookies, with consent

Free recipes on this site are paid for by advertising. If you accept advertising cookies, third party vendors including Google may set cookies on your device to select advertisements and to measure whether they were seen.

Google specifically

  • Third party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this website and other websites.
  • Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to you based on your visit to our site and other sites on the internet.
  • You may opt out of personalised advertising by visiting Google's Ads Settings page.
  • You may opt out of some third party vendors' use of cookies for personalised advertising through the industry opt out pages run by the Digital Advertising Alliance and the Network Advertising Initiative.

If you reject advertising cookies, you will still see advertising on the site. It will simply be selected without using your browsing history, which usually means it is less relevant to you and worth less to us. That is a trade we are happy for you to make.

6. Third party embedded content

We keep embedded third party content to a minimum. The site loads web fonts and photographs from third party services in order to display the pages. These requests do not set advertising cookies, but the providers will receive your IP address as part of any normal web request, as happens with any resource loaded from another domain.

We do not embed social media widgets, comment systems or video players that track you across sites.

7. Changing your choice

  1. On this site

    Select Cookie settings in the footer of any page. The banner reopens with the preference panel expanded and your current choice shown.

  2. In your browser

    All major browsers let you block or delete cookies for a specific site or for all sites. Look under Settings, then Privacy. Blocking essential storage may mean the cookie banner reappears on every visit, because we can no longer remember what you chose.

  3. Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

    Where your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, we treat it as a rejection of non-essential cookies.

8. Legal basis and your rights

Australian privacy law does not have a standalone cookie statute, but information collected through cookies can be personal information under the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), and where it is, the Australian Privacy Principles apply to it. We have chosen to ask for consent before setting non-essential cookies regardless, because it is the clearer and fairer approach.

You can ask what personal information we hold, ask us to correct it, or complain about how we have handled it. The process, including how to complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, is set out in the Privacy Policy.

9. Changes to this policy

If we add or remove a category of cookie, we update this page and the date at the top, and we ask for consent again where the change is material.
