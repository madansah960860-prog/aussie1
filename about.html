About Sunlit Pantry

An Australian recipe site written by one cook in one home kitchen, with metric measures, real testing and an open account of how it pays for itself.

Meet the cook

My name is Marlee Ashcroft. I cooked in Melbourne restaurant kitchens for around fifteen years, mostly in small places doing Mediterranean and modern Australian food, and I now write and test everything on this site from home.

Sunlit Pantry started because I kept being asked for recipes by friends and kept sending back messages that were three paragraphs long, full of the details a printed recipe leaves out. Why the pan has to be that hot. Why you salt at the end and not the start. What to do when it looks like it has split.

Restaurant cooking taught me technique and taught me speed. It did not teach me how to cook for four people on a Tuesday with a single oven, no salamander, no commis chef and a dishwasher that is also me. That gap is what this site is about.

How recipes are tested

Every recipe is cooked at least twice before it goes up, and most are cooked three or four times. Testing happens in a domestic kitchen with a standard 60 cm electric oven, a four burner gas cooktop, a four burner hooded gas barbecue and no professional equipment. If a recipe needs a stand mixer or a food processor, that is stated, along with what to do if you do not own one.

The oven in that kitchen runs about 12°C cool at 200°C, which we correct for during testing and mention because it is a useful reminder that your oven is probably out too.

Ingredients are bought from ordinary places: Coles, Woolworths, Aldi, the local greengrocer, the butcher on the corner and the Asian grocer two streets over. If a recipe needs something you can only get from a specialist supplier, we say where and we suggest a substitute.

When something does not work, it does not get published. There are about a dozen recipes that failed testing and are sitting in a folder, including three attempts at a gluten free lamington that were all structurally unsound.

What you can expect here

  • Metric measures by default, with an Australian cups toggle on every recipe.
  • Australian ingredient names and Australian retail context.
  • Oven temperatures in °C with the fan-forced equivalent beside them.
  • Prep, cook and total times, servings, difficulty, allergen tags, storage and make-ahead notes on every recipe.
  • No life story before the ingredients list. The recipe is at the top and there is a jump link in the bar.

How this site is funded

Recipes here are free and there is no paywall on any cooking content. Running the site costs money, so here is exactly where the money comes from, in plain English. The full detail is on our advertising and affiliate disclosure page.

  1. Display advertising

    We sell advertising space through advertising partners including Google. Ads are labelled, kept below the main content, and never placed on top of a recipe, a button or the navigation. There are no ads on our legal pages or the error page.

  2. Affiliate commissions

    Some links to kitchen equipment and pantry ingredients earn a small commission if you buy through them. Every one of those links carries a disclosure right next to it, not just in the footer, and the price you pay does not change.

  3. Paid cookbooks and meal plan bundles

    We sell downloadable PDF cookbooks and seasonal meal-plan bundles priced in Australian dollars, currently from $14.00 AUD. Buying one is optional and never removes anything from the free site.

  4. Optional membership

    Pantry Membership is $6.50 AUD a month for ad-free browsing, printable recipe cards and saved collections. Everything a member sees is also available free with ads.

  5. Sponsored and brand-partnered recipes

    Occasionally a brand pays us to develop a recipe. Those pages carry a sponsorship label at the very top, above the recipe, in line with Australian Consumer Law and ACCC guidance. We keep control of the method and the opinions.

  6. Newsletter sponsorship

    The weekly email sometimes carries a sponsor, clearly labelled inside the email. Details and rates are on the work with us page.

What we will not do

  • No health or medical claims. We are cooks, not dietitians or doctors. Nothing here is nutritional advice, and we do not describe food as healing, detoxifying, slimming or therapeutic. If you have a dietary or medical need, talk to a qualified health professional.
  • No invented nutrition figures presented as fact. Nutrition panels on this site are estimates and are labelled as such on every recipe.
  • No fake reviews or fabricated star ratings. If you see a rating on this site, it came from real readers, and where a figure is a placeholder during development it is marked in the page source.
  • No superlatives we cannot back up. You will not find best ever, number one, or guaranteed here. Those words mean nothing and we would rather describe what a dish actually tastes like.
  • No recipes we have not cooked. Everything is tested. Nothing is generated and published unread.

Corrections and feedback

If a recipe does not work for you, we want to know. Tell us what happened, what oven you used and where it went wrong, and we will retest. Genuine errors are corrected and the change is noted on the page rather than quietly edited out.

We also keep a list of recipe requests and work through it when planning the next round of testing. Requests for allergen-friendly versions of existing recipes are particularly useful, because those take the most testing and we would rather spend that time on something people actually want.
