Weekly Meal Plans

Five dinners built around a single shop, with the list already written and grouped by aisle. The plans are free, they use recipes that are already on the site, and there is nothing to sign up for.

How these plans are built

Every plan follows the same three rules. First, overlap the ingredients, so a bunch of coriander or half a jar of tahini gets used across two or three meals instead of going soft in the crisper. Second, front-load the effort, so the longest cook is on a night most people have time. Third, keep at least two dinners under thirty minutes, because a plan that assumes you have an hour every night is a plan you will abandon by Wednesday.

Quantities assume four adult serves per dinner. If you are cooking for two, most recipes on the site have a servings scaler that recalculates every quantity, and the leftovers of the longer cooks are usually better on day two anyway.

Costs are not listed, because grocery prices move constantly and vary between states and between Coles, Woolworths, Aldi and the local greengrocer. What the plans do instead is favour cheaper cuts, seasonal produce and pantry staples, which is where the real saving is.

The timeline

  • Sunday afternoon. Start the ragù. While it is in the oven, roast the whole pumpkin for Tuesday and make the tahini dressing for lunches.
  • Monday. Slice all the ginger at once and keep half in a container for Wednesday.
  • Tuesday. Blend the already roasted pumpkin. Twenty minutes, not fifty.
  • Wednesday. Glaze goes on in the last twelve minutes, not before.
  • Thursday. Salt the salmon skin as soon as you get home, then leave it while you do everything else.

Cooking for two, with lunches

Three cooks that cover five dinners and three lunches, using the servings scaler on each recipe page.

  • Sunday: beef ragù at full six serves. Two dinners plus two lunches.
  • Tuesday: warm grain bowl at four serves. One dinner plus two lunches.
  • Thursday: prawn and avocado rice bowls scaled to two.

Note. Do not scale the ragù down. It freezes well and the effort is identical, which is the whole point of cooking it.
