Every collection on the site

Four collections sorted by how you cook, and three sorted by what you feel like eating. Most recipes appear in two of them, because a charred vegetable platter is both a barbecue dish and a vegetarian one.

By occasion

Start here if the question is how much time you have and who you are feeding.

  • Quick Weeknight Dinners

    Five complete recipes, all on the table in forty minutes or less, with one pan and one board. Includes rice bowls, pasta salad, poached eggs, grilled salmon and bruschetta.

  • Baking & Desserts

    Seven complete recipes weighed in grams, with a fan-forced temperature beside every conventional one. Birthday cake, cupcakes, hotcakes, bread and three things that need no oven at all.

  • BBQ & Outdoor

    Five complete recipes written for a four burner hooded gas barbecue, including a whole snapper, pizza with the hood down, and the vegetable platter that saves the vegetarians.

  • Vegetarian & Vegan

    Six complete recipes with the vegan swap written out rather than left to you. Soup, salad, grain bowls, fruit, overnight oats and a smoothie that is actually thick.

By cuisine

Three ways of cooking that most Australian kitchens move between without thinking about it.

  • Modern Australian

    What actually gets cooked here: share plates, native ingredients used with some care, and a lot of things eaten outside.

  • Asian at Home

    A short pantry list that unlocks most weeknight cooking, and honest notes about what we are and are not qualified to write about.

  • Mediterranean

    Olive oil, acid, herbs and time. The style that suits the Australian climate and the Australian growing calendar better than almost anything else.

Other ways in

Collections are only one route through the site. If you would rather search by ingredient, filter by time or dietary need, or just see everything at once, the full index does all of that on one page.

If you are cooking for the week rather than for tonight, the meal plans group five dinners around a single shop, with the list already written and grouped by aisle.

  • Seasonal produce guide, written for Southern Hemisphere growing seasons where summer runs from December to February.
